# Cabin Crew Pre-Flight Safety Equipment Check

SAMPLE DOCUMENT — FICTIONAL. Written for testing the training system's chatbot. It is not a Royal Jordanian procedure and must not be used operationally.

Document reference: SOP-CC-014 (sample). Revision 3. Owner: Cabin Services Training.

## 1. Purpose

This procedure describes how cabin crew check the emergency and safety equipment on board before every flight. The aim is to make sure that every item is present, correctly stowed and serviceable before passengers board, and that any defect is reported in time for it to be fixed or formally deferred.

## 2. Scope

The procedure applies to all cabin crew operating any sector, including positioning flights where passengers are carried. It covers the first flight of the day and every subsequent sector, even when the crew does not change aircraft. It does not cover the technical checks carried out by licensed engineers.

## 3. Responsibilities

The Senior Cabin Crew Member (SCCM) assigns each crew member a check zone during the pre-flight briefing. Each crew member checks every item in their zone and reports the result to the SCCM. The SCCM collects all zone reports, confirms that the whole cabin has been checked, and reports the cabin status to the Captain.

Crew members are responsible only for their own zone, but anyone who notices a problem anywhere in the cabin must report it to the SCCM straight away.

## 4. Timing of the check

The check starts as soon as the crew boards the aircraft and the cabin is handed over by the cleaning team. All zone checks must be completed and reported to the SCCM at least 15 minutes before the planned boarding time.

Passenger boarding must not start until the SCCM has confirmed to the Captain that the safety equipment check and the security search are complete. If the check cannot be completed in time, the SCCM informs the Captain and the station duty manager so that boarding can be delayed.

## 5. Equipment checks by item

### 5.1 Portable oxygen bottles

Check that each bottle is in its bracket and the bracket is locked. Read the pressure gauge: the needle must be in the green band, which means at least 1,500 psi. Check that a mask is attached to the outlet and that its bag is sealed.

A bottle reading below 1,500 psi, or with a missing mask, is unserviceable. Tag it "U/S", leave it in place, and report it as described in section 7.

### 5.2 Fire extinguishers

Check that each halon extinguisher is in its stowage and the quick-release strap is fastened. The safety pin must be in place and its seal unbroken. The pressure gauge needle must be in the green band.

Each extinguisher carries an inspection tag. The date on the tag must be within the last 12 months. An extinguisher with an expired tag, a broken seal or a gauge outside the green band is unserviceable.

### 5.3 Smoke hoods (protective breathing equipment)

Smoke hoods are stored in a vacuum-sealed pack with a humidity indicator. Check that the pack is intact and the indicator is blue.

If the indicator has turned pink, or the vacuum seal is broken, the smoke hood is unserviceable. Remove it, replace it with a serviceable unit from the spare equipment stowage, and record the replacement in the Cabin Defect Log. Never open a smoke hood pack to inspect the hood inside.

### 5.4 First aid kits

Check that each first aid kit is in its stowage and the tamper seal is intact. The seal number must match the number recorded in the Cabin Defect Log. A kit with a broken seal or a mismatched number must be reported so the contents can be checked and the kit resealed.

### 5.5 Emergency torches

Each crew seat has an emergency torch in a bracket. Check that the torch is present and that its status light is flashing. A flashing light shows that the battery is charged. A torch with no flashing light is unserviceable.

### 5.6 Life jackets

Count the crew demonstration life jackets and the spare infant life jackets against the equipment list for the aircraft type. Passenger life jackets under the seats are checked by sampling: open the pouch on five randomly chosen seats in your zone and confirm that a life jacket is present.

### 5.7 Automated external defibrillator

On aircraft fitted with a defibrillator, check that it is in its stowage and that the status window shows a green tick. A red cross or a blank status window means the unit is unserviceable.

## 6. Emergency exits and slides

Before checking a door, make sure it is in disarmed (manual) mode. Check that the door area is clear of obstructions and that the assist handle is secure. Read the slide pressure gauge through the inspection window: the needle must be in the green band.

Do not operate or arm a door during the pre-flight check unless instructed by the SCCM.

## 7. Reporting unserviceable equipment

Any unserviceable or missing item must be recorded in the Cabin Defect Log and reported to the SCCM immediately. The SCCM informs the Captain and the station engineer.

Cabin crew never decide on their own whether the aircraft can depart with a defect. The Captain makes that decision, using the Minimum Equipment List (MEL) and advice from the engineer. If the item can be replaced from spares, as with a smoke hood, the crew member replaces it and still records the replacement in the log.

## 8. Security search

During the pre-flight check, each crew member also searches their zone for suspicious or unattended items. The search covers lavatories, overhead bins, seat pockets, crew rest areas and galley stowages.

If a suspicious item is found, do not touch or move it. Keep other people away from the area and report it to the SCCM at once. The SCCM informs the Captain, who will involve airport security.

## 9. Records

Each crew member signs the pre-flight checklist for their zone. The SCCM countersigns the completed checklist and hands it to the Captain with the cabin report. Signed checklists are kept by Cabin Services for 90 days.
